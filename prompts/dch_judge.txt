Evaluate each item independently using its audio and caption. You must actually have access to the waveform to label this an audio-grounded evaluation. If you only have reference captions/labels, identify your basis as reference_only and treat unsupported-by-reference claims as unreferenced, not verified absent.

Do not infer the correction method or compare versions of the same item. Do not read the method key. List only claims made about this recording: distinguish asserted sources/events from negations, examples of typical sources, and explicitly hypothetical explanations. A list of emergency vehicles that commonly use sirens does not assert that every listed vehicle is present.

Return one JSON object per item with:
- item_id
- basis: audio_grounded or reference_only
- unsupported_source_claims: each specific source identity confidently unsupported by the audio (or unreferenced if reference_only); include the caption span and evidence
- unsupported_sound_event_claims: each asserted audible event confidently unsupported by the audio (or unreferenced if reference_only); include the caption span and evidence
- uncertain_claims: ambiguous, inaudible or insufficiently annotated claims; do not force them into absent
- retained_supported_claims: supported content actually conveyed
- omitted_reference_claims: reference claims omitted, allowing paraphrases; do not treat annotations as exhaustive
- other_errors: clear action, order, count, timing, attribute or metadata errors, separate from source/event presence
- fluency: integer 1 to 5, judging grammatical readability
- informativeness: integer 1 to 5, judging correct useful detail

The source/event lists can overlap when an incorrect source identity also makes an event description wrong. Do not add their lengths and call that a total of unique errors. Do not penalize generic words such as atmosphere or seconds as hallucinated objects. Conversely, do not reward a nearly empty caption as an informative success. State uncertainty honestly.
